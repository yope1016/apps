Kitsuki: el pueblo samurái que te deja entrar gratis si vas en kimono

En una frase: a 15 minutos de Beppu en tren hay un pueblo-castillo donde el kimono no es disfraz, sino pase: si lo llevas puesto, entras gratis a las casas samurái, al museo y al castillo, y varios comercios te hacen descuento. Lo alquilas ahí mismo por 3,500 ¥ (con vestido incluido) y subes la cuesta más fotografiada de Kyūshū.

Por qué es especial

  • Un «pueblo sándwich»: la oficina de turismo lo llama el único «pueblo-castillo sándwich» de Japón. Los samuráis vivían en dos colinas (Kitadai al norte y Minamidai al sur) y los comerciantes en el valle de en medio (Tanimachi). Dos cuestas de piedra, Suya-no-saka y Shioya-no-saka, se miran de frente a través del valle: desde arriba ves las dos a la vez.
  • El kimono abre puertas: fue la primera ciudad reconocida como «paisaje histórico para vivir con kimono». Quien va en kimono no paga en las instalaciones turísticas públicas, y hay descuentos en restaurantes y tiendas participantes. El tercer sábado de cada mes hay «Kimono Kanshasai», un día de agradecimiento a quienes pasean en kimono.
  • El castillo más pequeño: el castillo de Kitsuki se fundó en 1394 sobre una colina junto al mar. La torre original ardió por un rayo en 1608; la actual, de tres pisos, es una reconstrucción de 1970, con vista a la bahía de Morie. Hasta 1712 el pueblo se escribía 木付: un documento del shōgun lo anotó como 杵築 y el error se quedó como nombre oficial.
  • Sabor local: pide ryūkyū, pescado crudo marinado en soya, sésamo y jengibre, típico de Ōita. En 2026 la Agencia de Cultura lo reconoció como «comida de 100 años».

Kitsuki: the samurai town that lets you in free if you wear a kimono

In one line: 15 minutes by train from Beppu is a castle town where a kimono isn't a costume but a pass: wear one and you get free entry to the samurai houses, the museum and the castle, plus discounts at many shops. Rent one right there for ¥3,500 (dressing included) and climb the most photographed slope in Kyūshū.

Why it's special

  • A "sandwich" town: the tourism office calls it Japan's only "sandwich castle town". Samurai lived on two hills (Kitadai to the north, Minamidai to the south) and merchants in the valley between (Tanimachi). Two stone slopes, Suya-no-saka and Shioya-no-saka, face each other across the valley: from the top you see both at once.
  • The kimono opens doors: it was the first town recognised as a "historic townscape to enjoy in kimono". Kimono wearers pay nothing at public tourist facilities and get discounts at participating restaurants and shops. On the third Saturday of every month there's "Kimono Kanshasai", a thank-you day for kimono walkers.
  • The smallest castle: Kitsuki Castle was founded in 1394 on a hill by the sea. The original keep burned after a lightning strike in 1608; today's three-storey keep is a 1970 reconstruction overlooking Morie Bay. Until 1712 the town was written 木付; a shogunate document wrote it as 杵築, and the mistake stuck as the official name.
  • Local taste: order ryūkyū, raw fish marinated in soy sauce, sesame and ginger, an Ōita classic. In 2026 the Agency for Cultural Affairs named it a "100-year food".

A half-day route

  1. 10:00: arrive at Kitsuki bus terminal and get dressed at Warakuan (book ahead; dressing takes a while).
  2. 11:00: climb Suya-no-saka to the northern samurai district. The classic photo: from the top, looking across to Shioya-no-saka.
  3. 12:00: lunch in Tanimachi (ask for ryūkyū and the kimono discount).
  4. 13:30: southern district: Ōhara Residence and Isoya house. Finish at the castle, with the sea view. Return the kimono before closing (16:00).

⚠️ Before you go

  • Facility fees changed on 1 April 2026; the prices here come from 2025–2026 guides and we could not read the new official fee table. Confirm them on the Kitsuki tourism site or on arrival.
  • Sources disagree on some houses' opening time (9:30 or 10:00) and on the taxi fare from the station (¥1,500–2,000).
  • Warakuan takes no general bookings on Monday 16 November 2026 (group booking). If it rains, umbrellas are available to rent.
  • The slopes are stone and slippery when wet: walk slowly in zōri sandals.

🚆 Getting there

  • From Beppu: JR Nippō Line to Kitsuki, about 15 min by limited express (¥1,060) or 25 min by local train (¥560). Then a bus to Kitsuki bus terminal, about 10 min (¥290, roughly hourly); the station is about 5 km from the centre.
  • From Ōita Airport: direct bus to Kitsuki bus terminal, about 30 min (¥720).
  • Combine with: the Kunisaki Peninsula (#09), Beppu's Kannawa "hells" (#90) or the Usuki stone Buddhas, in the same prefecture.
